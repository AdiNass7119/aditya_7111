About

Virat Kohli (born 5 November 1988)[b] is an Indian international cricketer who plays Test and ODI cricket for the national team and is a former captain in all formats.[5] He is a right-handed batsman and an occasional right arm medium pace bowler. He is called the king, the chase master and the run machine for his playing style, records and ability to lead the team to victory.[6] Kohli is the highest run-scorer in the Indian Premier League, third in T20I, third in ODI, and third in international cricket.[7] Regarded as one of the greatest batsmen of all time, he holds the record for the most ODI centuries and has the second most centuries in international cricket.[8] Kohli is also the only captain to lead India to three consecutive Test mace wins during 2017, 2018, and 2019.[9] Kohli led the Indian team to victory at the 2008 U19 World Cup and was a key member of the teams that won the 2011 ODI World Cup, 2013 Champions Trophy, 2024 T20 World Cup and 2025 Champions Trophy. He represents Royal Challengers Bengaluru in the Indian Premier League and Delhi in domestic cricket. In 2013, Kohli was ranked number one in the ODI batting rankings. In 2015, he achieved the same in T20I.[10] In 2018, he was ranked the number one Test batsman, making him the only Indian to hold the number one spot in all three formats. He is the first player to score 20,000 runs in a decade. ICC named him the male cricketer of the decade for the years 2011 to 2020.[11] Kohli has garnered 10 ICC Awards making him the most awarded player in international cricket history. He won the ODI Player of the Year award four times in 2012, 2017, 2018, and 2023. He has also won the Sir Garfield Sobers Trophy given to the Cricketer of the Year, on two occasions, in 2017 and 2018. In 2018, he touched his peak and became the first player to win all the three major awards Garfield Sobers trophy, ODI and Test Player of the Year awards in the same year. He was awarded with Spirit of Cricket Award in 2019. He was also awarded with Cricketer of the Decade and ODI Cricketer of the Decade in 2020. Kohli was named the Wisden Leading Cricketer in the World for three consecutive years, from 2016 to 2018. Kohli has the most Player of the Series awards and second most Player of the Match awards to his name in all three formats combined. He was honoured with the Arjuna Award in 2013, the Padma Shri in 2017, and India's highest sporting honour, the Khel Ratna Award, in 2018. Time magazine included him on its list of the 100 most influential people in the world in 2018. After winning the 2024 T20 World Cup and winning the Player of the Match award in the final, Kohli announced his retirement from T20Is.[12] He is married to actress Anushka Sharma and they have two children

Early life

Kohli was born on 5 November 1988 in Delhi into a Punjabi Hindu family. His mother Saroj Kohli is as a housewife while his father Prem Nath Kohli worked as a criminal lawyer. He has an elder brother Vikas and an elder sister Bhawna.[14] His formative years were spent in Uttam Nagar. His early education was at Vishal Bharti Public School.[15] As per his family, Kohli exhibited an early affinity for cricket as a 3-year-old. He would pick up a bat and request his father bowl to him.[16] In 1998, the West Delhi Cricket Academy was created. In May, his father arranged for him to meet Rajkumar Sharma.[1] Upon the suggestion of their neighbours, Kohli's father considered enrolling his son in a professional cricket academy, as they believed his ability merited more than gully cricket.[17] He was unable to secure a place in the U-14 Delhi team, due to extraneous factors. His father reportedly received offers to relocate his son to influential clubs, which would ensure his selection, but he declined the proposals. Kohli found his way into the U-15 team.[1] He received training at the academy and participated in matches at the Sumeet Dogra Academy located at Vasundhara Enclave.[18] In pursuit of furthering his cricketing career, he transferred to Saviour Convent School during his ninth-grade education.[17] On 18 December 2006, his father died due to a cerebral attack.[17][15] As per his mother, Kohli's demeanour shifted noticeably after his father's death. He took on cricket with newfound seriousness, prioritizing playing time and dedicating himself fully to the sport.[17] Kohli's family resided in Meera Bagh, Paschim Vihar until the year 2015, after which they relocated to Gurgaon.[19]

Debut and maiden stint (2008–2009)

In 2008, Kohli was included in the ODl squad for the Sri Lanka tour and the Champions Trophy.[39] He was required to fill the role of makeshift opener throughout the Sri Lankan series.[40] On August 18, 2008, Kohli made his international debut at the age of 19 in the first ODI of the tour.[41] In the 4th match of the series, Kohli scored his first half century in ODl consisting of fifty-four runs.[42] He was picked in the India A squad for the Tests against Australia A in September.[43] Despite limited opportunities, he managed to make an impact in the single innings that he participated in, scoring 49 runs.[44] In Oct, Kohli participate in a four-day tour match against Australia as part of the Indian Board President's XI team. The match featured a formidable Australian bowling line-up that consisted of Brett Lee, Stuart Clark, Mitchell Johnson, Peter Siddle and Jason Krejza. Despite this, Kohli scored 105 runs in the first innings, showing his ability to perform against top international competition.[45] In December, Kohli was awarded a Grade D contract by the Board of Control for Cricket in India as part of the annual contract list for the Indian national team which entitled him to receive ₹1.5 million (equivalent to ₹4.2 million or US$49,000 in 2023).[46] In 2009, Kohli was selected in the four-team Emerging Players Tournament in Australia. He went on to have a standout performance and finished as the tournament's leading run-scorer, with a total of 398 runs from seven matches, at an average of 66.33. In the final match, he scored 104 runs off 102 balls against the South Africa Emerging Players in Brisbane. His strong performance helped lead his team to a 17-run victory and the tournament title.[47][48] Kris Srikkanth, the chairman of the Indian national selection committee, expressed his admiration for his performance during the tournament.[49] Kohli later stated that this tournament was a "turning point" in his career.[50] In August, Kohli was a part of the Indian squad for the tri-series in Sri Lanka.[51] He was utilized as a middle order batsman in the 2009 Champions Trophy.[52] In December, he was included in the team for the ODI series against Sri Lanka and scored 27 and 54 in the first two ODIs.[53] Kohli scored his maiden ODI century, 107 off 114 balls while sharing a 224 run partnership with Gautam Gambhir. As a result, India won by seven wickets and sealed the series 3–1

Achievements

Virat Kohli, born on November 5, 1988, in Delhi, India, has etched his name in cricket history through a combination of talent, dedication, and an insatiable hunger for excellence. From his early days, Kohli showcased a prodigious talent that would later blossom into one of the most illustrious careers in international cricket.​ Kohli's journey began in earnest when he captained the Indian Under-19 team to victory in the 2008 ICC Under-19 Cricket World Cup. This triumph served as a springboard, propelling him into the senior national team later that year. Making his ODI debut against Sri Lanka in August 2008, Kohli quickly established himself as a reliable top-order batsman. His first ODI century came in December 2009 against Sri Lanka, marking the beginning of a prolific run-scoring spree.​ In the 2011 ICC Cricket World Cup, Kohli played a pivotal role in India's triumph, scoring a century in the opening match against Bangladesh and contributing crucial runs throughout the tournament. His ability to chase targets became legendary, with numerous match-winning innings under pressure. By 2023, Kohli had amassed 51 ODI centuries, surpassing Sachin Tendulkar's record for the most centuries in the format. Notably, he achieved his 50th ODI century during the World Cup semi-final against New Zealand, further cementing his status as one of the game's greats.​ Kohli's Test career has been equally impressive. Debuting in 2011 against the West Indies, he faced initial challenges but soon adapted to the rigors of the longer format. His maiden Test century came in January 2012 against Australia in Adelaide. Over the years, he accumulated 30 Test centuries, including seven double centuries, showcasing his ability to play long, impactful innings. His aggressive yet calculated approach redefined India's batting ethos in Test cricket.​ Wikipedia In T20 Internationals, Kohli has been a consistent performer. He scored his maiden T20I century in 2022 and played a crucial role in India's T20 World Cup campaigns. In 2024, he announced his retirement from T20Is after leading India to a World Cup victory, leaving behind a legacy of numerous match-winning performances.​ Kohli's leadership qualities came to the fore when he took over the Test captaincy in 2014, following MS Dhoni's retirement from the format. Under his stewardship, India achieved the number one ranking in Test cricket and secured memorable series victories, including a historic win in Australia in 2018-19. His tenure as captain was marked by an emphasis on fitness, aggression, and a never-say-die attitude.​ In the Indian Premier League (IPL), Kohli has been synonymous with the Royal Challengers Bangalore (RCB) franchise. He holds the record for the most runs in IPL history, with over 8,000 runs to his name. His 2016 season was particularly remarkable, where he scored 973 runs, including four centuries, the most by any player in a single IPL season. Despite RCB not clinching the title, Kohli's individual performances have been a highlight of the tournament.​ Beyond the numbers, Kohli's commitment to fitness revolutionized the Indian team's approach to physical conditioning. His emphasis on discipline and work ethic set new benchmarks for professionalism in Indian cricket. Off the field, he has been involved in various philanthropic activities through the Virat Kohli Foundation, focusing on child welfare and animal rights. His marriage to Bollywood actress Anushka Sharma in 2017 has also made them one of India's most celebrated couples.​ Kohli's accolades are numerous. He has been honored with the Arjuna Award (2013), Padma Shri (2017), and Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna (2018), among others. In 2020, he was named the ICC Men's Cricketer of the Decade, a testament to his dominance across formats. His influence extends beyond cricket, with a significant presence in endorsements and social media, making him one of the most marketable athletes globally.​ As of April 2025, Kohli continues to be a vital cog in India's cricketing machinery. His recent performances in the 2025 Champions Trophy, including a match-winning century against Pakistan, underscore his enduring class and hunger for success. With over 27,000 international runs and counting, Kohli's journey is a testament to what relentless passion and hard work can achieve. His story serves as an inspiration to aspiring cricketers worldwide, embodying the spirit of perseverance and excellence.

Virat Kohli’s legacy as one of the most complete batsmen across all formats is underlined by his adaptability and technique. Whether it’s the bouncy tracks in Australia, the swinging conditions of England, or the spinning pitches in the subcontinent, Kohli has proven his mettle in all circumstances. His ability to convert starts into big scores has been a hallmark of his game, especially in pressure situations. His success in that series was seen as the final frontier in proving his dominance across all playing conditions. The tour also highlighted his leadership skills, as he remained India's top performer in a difficult series. In Australia, his 2014 series saw him score four centuries in four Tests, despite the team's overall struggles, further elevating his stature as a modern-day great. As a captain, Kohli’s win percentage in Test matches is among the highest for any Indian skipper. Under his leadership, India remained undefeated in Test series at home for over six years. He instilled a sense of fearlessness in the team and emphasized fast bowling as a primary weapon, resulting in the emergence of a formidable pace attack featuring Jasprit Bumrah, Mohammed Shami, and Ishant Sharma. This focus on pace contributed significantly to India’s overseas successes, particularly in Australia and South Africa. Virat’s passion for Test cricket helped revive interest in the format in a country increasingly tilted toward T20s. He constantly advocated for the importance of red-ball cricket and was instrumental in popularizing the ICC World Test Championship. His animated and emotional style on the field resonated with fans and brought a unique energy to the game. His respect for the traditions of the sport, combined with his modern approach, made him a bridge between the classical and contemporary cricketing eras. In the IPL, while he has not yet lifted the trophy, his loyalty to RCB and consistent performances have made him a fan favorite. His leadership has often been credited with building a strong core around young Indian players, helping groom future stars like Devdutt Padikkal and Mohammed Siraj. Even when not captaining, his presence in the dressing room is influential, and he continues to serve as a mentor and motivator. Kohli’s influence extends far beyond the boundary rope. He is one of the most followed athletes on social media, with millions of fans across platforms. He uses his platform to promote fitness, mental health awareness, and animal rights. The "Virat Kohli Foundation" has partnered with several NGOs and works toward providing opportunities to underprivileged children, especially in sports and education. His image as a global sports icon is built not just on performances but also on his character, charisma, and willingness to stand up for causes he believes in. Fitness has become a central theme of his public persona. After struggling with form and injuries early in his career, Kohli underwent a complete transformation in 2015 by adopting a strict diet and fitness routine. He credited this shift for extending his peak performance years and encouraging younger players to take fitness seriously. Today, the Indian cricket team follows a regime influenced heavily by Kohli’s philosophy, focusing on agility, strength, and endurance. Kohli’s awards and honors continue to grow. He has been featured multiple times in the Forbes list of highest-paid athletes and was included in Time magazine's list of the 100 most influential people in the world. He has received multiple ICC Player of the Month and Player of the Year awards and was the fastest to reach multiple batting milestones including 8000, 9000, 10000, 11000, and 12000 ODI runs. In addition to his accomplishments on the field, Kohli's life off the field has captivated the public. His marriage to actress Anushka Sharma brought together two of India’s biggest celebrities, and the couple is known for maintaining a balance between fame and privacy. Their public appearances are often lauded for their grace and simplicity, and they have used their platform to advocate for social change, environmental consciousness, and wellness. As of 2025, even with nearly two decades in international cricket, Kohli shows no signs of slowing down. He continues to evolve his game, focusing on mentorship, building partnerships, and guiding the next generation of cricketers. His recent performances in the ICC Champions Trophy and the India-Australia Test series suggest that he still has the hunger to perform at the highest level. His contributions are not only measured in runs and records but also in the legacy he leaves behind — one of discipline, passion, resilience, and excellence.

Educational_ife

Virat Kohli's educational life is closely tied to his early journey in cricket, as his passion for the sport took center stage from a very young age. Virat was born and raised in Uttam Nagar, Delhi, where he began his schooling at Vishal Bharti Public School. From the beginning, he was known to be a bright and enthusiastic student, but it was clear that his interests lay more on the cricket field than in the classroom. His teachers and peers noticed his exceptional commitment to sports, especially cricket, which often kept him away from regular academic routines. At the age of 9, recognizing his immense potential, his family enrolled him in the West Delhi Cricket Academy (WDCA), founded by Rajkumar Sharma, who later became a mentor in shaping Virat’s technique and mindset. Around the same time, he moved to Saviour Convent School in Paschim Vihar to balance his academics and cricket training, but cricket increasingly became his main focus. As his training intensified and he started representing Delhi in youth cricket tournaments, Kohli couldn't pursue higher education in the traditional sense. He eventually prioritized his sports career over academics, especially after his selection to the Delhi Under-15 and Under-17 teams and later the Indian Under-19 team. While Virat Kohli may not have pursued formal higher education like college or university, his life and career have been an education in themselves, marked by discipline, leadership, and learning through experiences on the field. Today, he often speaks about the importance of focus, time management, and perseverance—skills he developed early while balancing school and his demanding cricket schedule.

Marriage

Virat Kohli, the iconic Indian cricketer, and Anushka Sharma, one of Bollywood’s most successful actresses, form a power couple whose love story has captivated millions across the country. Their journey began in 2013 when the two met for the first time while shooting a television commercial for a shampoo brand. The advertisement not only marked the beginning of a successful campaign but also sparked the beginning of a beautiful relationship. Both were at the peak of their respective careers—Virat, a rising cricketing star and future Indian captain, and Anushka, a leading lady in the film industry known for her versatile roles and strong screen presence. Over the next few years, the couple was often spotted together at social gatherings, airports, and even cricket matches, but they kept their relationship largely private and away from media speculation. Despite being constantly in the limelight, they handled their relationship with maturity and discretion, never confirming or denying rumors during the initial years. However, the media and fans couldn’t help but take notice of their chemistry. In 2014, Anushka was seen in the stands supporting Virat during various cricket matches, and the couple slowly began to appear more publicly. One of the most talked-about moments came when Virat blew a kiss to Anushka after scoring a half-century in an ODI match, confirming the relationship for many. Despite facing public scrutiny—especially when India lost matches and some fans unfairly targeted Anushka—they stood by each other with unwavering support. Their relationship wasn’t just about glamor but also about substance, mutual respect, and understanding. Virat often credited Anushka for bringing balance and calmness to his life, while Anushka praised Virat for his honesty, discipline, and strong values. They both valued privacy and chose not to make their personal life a constant topic in the public domain. After four years of courtship, Virat and Anushka decided to take the plunge. On December 11, 2017, they got married in a dreamy and intimate ceremony at Borgo Finocchieto, a luxury heritage villa in Tuscany, Italy. The wedding was a private affair, attended by only close family members and a few friends. Everything about the ceremony reflected simplicity, elegance, and grace—from Anushka’s pastel pink Sabyasachi lehenga to the stunning Tuscan backdrop. The couple kept the wedding a secret until the very day, releasing official wedding photos on social media which instantly went viral and won hearts across the globe. Their choice to marry away from the media glare was appreciated by many, as it allowed them to celebrate their love quietly and authentically. Following the wedding, the couple hosted two grand receptions. The first was held in Delhi on December 21, 2017, and was attended by politicians, including Prime Minister Narendra Modi, who personally congratulated the couple. The second was held in Mumbai on December 26 and saw a star-studded turnout of cricketers, Bollywood celebrities, and industrialists. Post-marriage, the couple settled into their new life with grace. Despite their demanding careers, they continued to support each other professionally and personally. Anushka was often seen cheering for Virat during major tournaments, including World Cups and IPL seasons, while Virat made appearances at Anushka’s movie premieres and shoots. The two have also collaborated on various initiatives, including environmental causes and social awareness campaigns. Their Instagram posts often give glimpses into their life—cozy home dinners, vacations, festive celebrations, and shared moments of joy and laughter. In 2020, during the global pandemic, the couple spent significant time together in quarantine, giving fans more candid glimpses of their daily routines and bonding. They also jointly raised funds for COVID relief and continued their philanthropic efforts through their respective foundations. On January 11, 2021, Virat and Anushka welcomed their first child, a baby girl named Vamika. The announcement was made via a heartfelt note on social media, expressing their joy and requesting privacy. Since then, the couple has been very protective about their daughter’s privacy, choosing not to reveal her face or allow media coverage around her. Virat has often spoken about how fatherhood changed him, giving him a deeper sense of purpose and calmness. Anushka, too, took a brief break from acting to focus on motherhood and has since made a strong comeback while balancing family and work with elegance. Together, they have embraced parenthood while maintaining their respective careers, inspiring many with their partnership and ability to adapt to new phases in life. Their marriage is a rare blend of fame, love, and companionship. What makes their bond so special is the respect they have for each other’s individuality and the space they give one another to grow. Whether it’s supporting each other’s professional choices, standing strong through public criticism, or nurturing a family in the public eye, they’ve handled every chapter of their journey with sincerity. As of 2025, Virat and Anushka continue to remain one of the most admired celebrity couples in India, symbolizing love, partnership, and strength. Their story is not just about a cricketer marrying an actress—it’s about two driven individuals who have come together, grown stronger with time, and continue to thrive together as equals.
